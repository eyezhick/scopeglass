How to read these experiments

Garden paths · NP/Z. Cross an optionally transitive verb with an intransitive control, and a comma with no comma. At the main verb, subtract the control's comma benefit from the ambiguous verb's comma benefit. A positive interaction is compatible with garden-path sensitivity.

Agreement. Cross subject number, distractor number, and verb number. The preference is surprisal(wrong verb) − surprisal(correct verb). The attraction penalty is the loss of this preference when the distractor's number mismatches the subject.

Polarity. Cross negation presence, its location, and ever/often. Measure how much negation changes the preference for ever over often. Scope selectivity is the matrix benefit minus the embedded benefit.

NP/S ambiguity. Compare verbs that admit a noun-phrase object or a sentential complement with a clause-selecting control, with and without that. At the embedded verb, subtract the control's complementizer benefit from the ambiguous verb's benefit. A positive interaction is compatible with sensitivity to the temporary object/clause ambiguity; lexical and verb-selection differences remain possible explanations.

These are small, authored English materials. Bootstrap intervals describe this item set, not all language. Verb frequency, plausibility, punctuation, lexical choice, and training overlap remain possible confounds. Surprisal is behavioral evidence; it does not establish that a model represents a particular parse, or that people would show the same effect. Spillover is displayed but not scored.
