How to read these experiments

Garden paths. Cross an optionally transitive verb with an intransitive control, and a comma with no comma. At the main verb, subtract the control's comma benefit from the ambiguous verb's comma benefit. A positive interaction is compatible with garden-path sensitivity.

Agreement. Cross subject number, distractor number, and verb number. The preference is surprisal(wrong verb) − surprisal(correct verb). The attraction penalty is the loss of this preference when the distractor's number mismatches the subject.

Polarity. Cross negation presence, its location, and ever/often. Measure how much negation changes the preference for ever over often. Scope selectivity is the matrix benefit minus the embedded benefit.

These are small, authored English materials: 12 frames per experiment. Bootstrap intervals describe this item set, not all language. Verb frequency, plausibility, punctuation, lexical choice, and training overlap remain possible confounds. Surprisal is behavioral evidence; it does not establish that a model represents a particular parse, or that people would show the same effect. Spillover is displayed but not scored.
